### Snow days and emergency closures

Kamloops winters produce closures most years. The university announces closures by 06:00 for day classes and by 15:00 for evening classes. Announcements go out through the myTRU alert system, campus email, and text message for anyone enrolled in TRU Alert. The Williams Lake campus may close independently of Kamloops.

A closure does not cancel coursework. Instructors are expected to keep the course moving asynchronously through Moodle, and may not require synchronous attendance during a closure. Missed in person assessments are rescheduled, not dropped, unless the instructor chooses to drop them for the whole class.

If a closure falls on a final exam day, the affected exams shift to the study day or to the following Saturday. The Registrar publishes the revised schedule within twelve hours.

Wildfire smoke closures follow the same process during the summer sessions.

## Academic Integrity and Plagiarism Casework Guide

Thompson Rivers University, Kamloops. Office of Academic Integrity. Version 4.2, effective January 5, 2026.

Sample data. This document is demonstration content written for a prototype assistant. It is not real TRU policy. Confirm anything consequential with the responsible office.

This guide tells instructors what to do from the moment they suspect a violation to the moment the case is closed. It applies to on campus courses in Kamloops and Williams Lake, and to Open Learning courses.

### What counts as an academic integrity violation

Seven categories are recognized. An instructor does not need to decide which category applies before reporting. The Office of Academic Integrity will classify the case.

1. **Plagiarism.** Presenting another person's words, ideas, data, code, images, or structure as your own without attribution. This includes copying without quotation marks even when a citation is present, and paraphrasing closely without credit.
2. **Self plagiarism.** Submitting work that was already graded in another course without the written permission of both instructors.
3. **Unauthorized collaboration.** Working with others on an assessment the instructor designated as individual work.
4. **Contract cheating.** Buying, commissioning, trading, or otherwise obtaining work produced by someone else, including essay mills, tutoring services that produce finished work, and paid coding services.
5. **Exam and quiz misconduct.** Using prohibited materials, communicating during an assessment, taking an assessment for another student, or accessing questions in advance.
6. **Falsification.** Fabricating data, sources, citations, lab results, attendance records, or medical documentation.
7. **Unauthorized generative AI use.** Using an AI system in a way the course outline prohibits. See the section on AI cases below.

### The three case levels

Every case is handled at one of three levels. The level determines who resolves it, what sanctions are available, and how long the record is kept.

**Level 1. Instructor resolved.**
For a first offence that is limited in scope and appears to come from carelessness or misunderstanding rather than intent. Typical examples are a missing citation on one paragraph, an improperly quoted block, or a mishandled group assignment where boundaries were genuinely unclear.
The instructor resolves the case directly with the student. The instructor still files a report, but the outcome is recorded as an educational finding rather than a disciplinary one. Available sanctions are a warning, a required revision, a required integrity tutorial, or a grade reduction on the assignment up to and including a zero.
A Level 1 finding does not appear on the transcript and is not reported to graduate schools or professional bodies.

**Level 2. Formal case, managed by the Office of Academic Integrity.**
For a repeat offence, a violation that affects a substantial portion of an assessment, or any violation that appears intentional. Contract cheating and exam misconduct always start at Level 2 or higher.
The instructor reports and then steps back. A case officer investigates, meets the student, and issues a finding. Available sanctions include a zero on the assessment, a reduced course grade, a failing course grade, an integrity probation notation, and a required integrity course.
A Level 2 finding creates an internal disciplinary record kept for seven years after graduation.

**Level 3. Hearing panel.**
For a third offence of any kind, for falsification of academic records, for impersonation in an assessment, or for any case where suspension or expulsion is a possible outcome.
A three member panel drawn from the University Integrity Board hears the case. The panel includes one faculty member from outside the student's faculty, one faculty member from a related discipline, and one trained student member. Sanctions include everything available at Level 2 plus suspension for one or more terms, permanent transcript notation, and expulsion.

### What to do when you suspect a violation, step by step

**Step 1. Preserve the evidence before you do anything else.**
Download the submission file, export the similarity report, screenshot the Moodle submission timestamp, and save any relevant version history. Do not edit the student's submission. Do not delete a resubmission. If the assessment was on paper, scan it the same day.

**Step 2. Do not accuse the student in writing or in class.**
Do not email a student the word "plagiarism," "cheating," or "misconduct." Do not enter a grade of zero yet. Do not discuss the case with other students, with the student's advisor, or with family. An accusation made before a finding exposes the university and the instructor personally.

**Step 3. Hold an inquiry meeting within ten business days.**
Invite the student to a short, non adversarial meeting. Say that you have questions about the assignment and want to hear how they approached it. Ask process questions rather than accusatory ones. Useful questions are: walk me through how you researched this, where did you find this source, can you show me your drafts or notes, explain this paragraph in your own words.
The student may bring a support person who does not speak on their behalf. The meeting may be held online, which is the norm for Open Learning courses. If the student does not respond within five business days of your invitation, proceed without the meeting and note the non response in your report.

**Step 4. Decide whether there is a reasonable basis.**
The standard at this stage is not proof. It is whether a reasonable instructor, looking at the evidence, would find a violation more likely than not. If yes, report. If no, close it and keep your notes for one term.

**Step 5. File the report within fifteen business days of discovery.**
File through the Academic Integrity Reporting Form on myTRU, under Teaching, then Integrity Cases. The form takes about twenty minutes. Attach the preserved evidence. Describe what you observed factually and avoid characterizing intent.
The fifteen day clock starts when you first form the suspicion, not when you finish investigating. A late report does not void the case, but the delay must be documented and the student may raise it on appeal.

**Step 6. Hold the grade.**
Enter a placeholder of **PI**, which means pending integrity, in the Moodle gradebook. Do not enter a zero and do not submit a final course grade while a case is open. If the term ends first, submit a grade of **IP**, in progress, for the course. The Registrar will convert it once the case closes.

**Step 7. Wait for the finding, then apply the sanction.**
You are notified in writing. Apply only the sanction stated in the finding. Do not add an additional penalty of your own.

### Common case types and how they are usually handled

**Patchwriting and close paraphrase.** The student cites the source but the sentences track the original too closely. First offence with a cited source is normally Level 1. The usual outcome is a required revision and a grade reduction on the assignment. Teach the difference rather than punishing it heavily. This is the single most common case involving students writing in an additional language.

**Missing quotation marks around a cited passage.** Normally Level 1 on a first offence. If the passage is long or repeated across the paper, the case moves to Level 2.

**Copied from an uncited web source.** Level 2 if the copied material is a substantial part of the submission. Level 1 if it is one short passage in an otherwise original paper.

**Reused work from a previous course.** Level 1 if the courses were taken in consecutive terms and the course outline was silent on reuse. Level 2 if the outline explicitly prohibited reuse.

**Two students submit near identical work.** Report both. Do not decide who copied whom. That determination belongs to the case officer, who has access to submission timestamps, draft history, and prior case records that you do not.

**Purchased or commissioned work.** Level 2 minimum. Strong signals are a sudden and unexplained jump in writing quality, references the student cannot locate, formatting inconsistent with the rest of the course, metadata naming another author, and an inability to explain the argument in conversation. A similarity score is usually low in these cases, so do not rely on it.

**Exam misconduct observed during a proctored session.** Note the time, seat, and behaviour. Allow the student to finish the exam. Removing a student mid exam creates a due process problem. Collect the materials separately and report the same day.

**Group project where one member did not contribute.** This is a grading matter, not an integrity matter, unless a member falsified a contribution log. Handle it through your rubric.

### Generative AI cases

AI use is judged against what the course outline permits. There is no single campus wide rule, so the course outline statement is the governing document.

**If the course outline is silent on AI, an AI case cannot be sustained.** The case will be declined and the instructor asked to add a statement for the next term. This is the single most common reason AI reports fail. Check your outline before reporting.

**AI detector scores are not evidence.** TRU does not accept detector output, including Turnitin AI writing indicators, as the basis for a finding. Detectors produce false positives at rates high enough to be unusable, and they are documented to flag multilingual writers and neurodivergent writers more often. This matters here, since a large share of students write in an additional language.

**What does support an AI case:** the student's own admission, an inability to explain their submission in an oral follow up, fabricated citations that do not exist, pasted content that includes AI interface artifacts, Moodle version history showing the whole document appearing in a single paste, or a submission that answers a different prompt than the one assigned.

**The oral follow up is the recommended tool.** Ask the student to explain three specific choices in the work. A student who wrote it can do this. Document the conversation with dates and quotes.

**Fabricated citations are the strongest single signal.** Verify two or three references through the TRU Library. If they do not exist, that is falsification under category six regardless of how the text was produced, and the case is straightforward.

### Similarity reports, and how to read them

TRU licenses Turnitin. It is enabled by default on Moodle assignment submissions.

A similarity percentage is a measure of matched text, not a measure of plagiarism. Read the report, do not read the number.

- Matches to the assignment prompt, the reference list, quoted material, and standard method sections are normal and should be filtered out before you judge.
- A paper at 40 percent that is all correctly quoted and cited material has no violation.
- A paper at 8 percent where that 8 percent is the entire argument, uncited, is a serious violation.
- Exclude bibliography and quoted material in the report filters, then re read the score.
- Matches to another TRU student's paper from a prior term appear as institutional matches and should always be examined.

There is no threshold percentage that triggers a report. Do not put a percentage threshold in your course outline.

### Student rights during a case

Students are entitled to written notice of the allegation and the evidence, a meeting before any finding, a support person of their choosing, continued enrolment and attendance while the case is open, an outcome in writing with reasons, and one appeal.

A student may not be barred from class, removed from a group, or given a zero while a case is pending.

Students may seek independent advice from the TRU Students' Union advocacy service at any point, and instructors should tell them so.

### Appeals

A student may appeal a Level 2 or Level 3 finding within ten business days. Level 1 findings are not appealable, which is part of why Level 1 exists.

Appeals succeed on three grounds only: a procedural error that affected the outcome, new evidence that was not reasonably available at the time, or a sanction disproportionate to the finding. Disagreement with the finding itself is not a ground.

Appeals are decided by the Associate Vice President Academic, or by a different hearing panel for Level 3 cases. The instructor may be asked for a written response but does not attend the appeal.

### Prevention that actually works

- Change assessment prompts every term. Recycled prompts are the largest driver of contract cheating.
- Ask for a process artifact along with the product: an annotated bibliography, a proposal, a draft, a reflection.
- Assess in stages rather than in one high stakes submission.
- Include one low stakes oral or in class component in every major assignment.
- State your AI policy in the course outline in specific terms. Say what is allowed, what is not, and how to disclose use.
- Say out loud in week one that you would rather grant an extension than receive dishonest work, and mean it.

## Grading, Assessment and Grade Appeals Policy

Thompson Rivers University. Office of the Provost and Vice President Academic. Version 7.1, effective January 5, 2026.

Grading is a faculty responsibility. The university does not overturn an instructor's academic judgment. What the university does review is whether the grade was arrived at fairly, by the process the course outline described, and without error. This policy sets out that process.

### The grade scale

| Grade | Grade points | Percentage | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A+ | 4.33 | 90 to 100 | Outstanding |
| A | 4.00 | 85 to 89 | Excellent |
| A- | 3.67 | 80 to 84 | Excellent |
| B+ | 3.33 | 77 to 79 | Very good |
| B | 3.00 | 73 to 76 | Good |
| B- | 2.67 | 70 to 72 | Good |
| C+ | 2.33 | 67 to 69 | Satisfactory |
| C | 2.00 | 63 to 66 | Satisfactory |
| C- | 1.67 | 60 to 62 | Marginal, credit granted |
| D | 1.00 | 50 to 59 | Minimal pass, credit granted |
| F | 0.00 | below 50 | Fail, no credit |

The percentage bands are the university standard. An instructor may set different cutoffs only with department chair approval, and only if stated in the course outline before the term begins and applied consistently to the whole class.

A grade of C- or better is required to satisfy a prerequisite. Graduate courses require B- or better for credit.

### Administrative grades

| Symbol | Meaning | Affects GPA |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Incomplete | No, until it converts |
| IP | In progress, integrity case open | No |
| W | Withdrawn, student initiated by the deadline | No |

| CR and NCR | Credit and no credit | No |
| AUD | Audit, no credit | No |
| DEF | Deferred examination approved | No |

**NR is not a grade.** It appears when an instructor does not submit by the deadline. It blocks the student's transcript, student aid, and academic standing review, and it is visible to the dean. It converts to F after thirty days if no grade is entered.

### The incomplete grade, I

An I is for a student who has completed most of the coursework at a passing level and is prevented from finishing by a documented circumstance outside their control. Illness, a death in the family, and a serious personal emergency qualify. Poor time management, a heavy workload, and a wish to raise a grade do not.

Conditions:

- The student must have completed at least seventy percent of the graded work.
- The student must be passing the course at the time of the request.
- The instructor and student must file an Incomplete Contract in myTRU before the grade deadline. The contract states exactly what work remains, the deadline for it, and the grade the student earns if the work is never submitted.
- An I is never granted retroactively after final grades post.

Deadlines to clear an incomplete:

| Term the I was granted | Work due to the instructor | Grade change due to the Registrar |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fall 2026 | Friday, March 12, 2027 | Friday, March 26, 2027 |
| Winter 2026 | Friday, October 9, 2026 | Friday, October 23, 2026 |
| Summer 2026 | Friday, December 4, 2026 | Friday, December 18, 2026 |

An I that is not cleared converts automatically to the default grade on the contract, or to F if no default was stated. A single extension of one additional term may be requested from the dean before the deadline passes.

### Late work and extensions

Late penalties must be in the course outline. A penalty that is not published cannot be applied.

Every course outline must state a late work policy, even if that policy is that no late work is accepted. Silence is treated as accepting late work without penalty if a student appeals.

Instructors are encouraged, not required, to build in flexibility: a stated number of no questions asked extension days, a lowest score dropped, or a revision option. Departments have found this reduces integrity cases and reduces accommodation requests.

An instructor may not apply a late penalty to work that is late because of an approved accommodation, an approved religious observance, an approved athletic or academic travel absence, or a documented university closure. Kamloops snow closures and summer wildfire smoke closures both fall under the last category.

### Curving and scaling

TRU neither requires nor forbids curving. If you scale, three rules apply.

1. Scaling may only raise grades, never lower them.
2. The method must be stated in the course outline or announced to the whole class in writing before the assessment.
3. A quota curve, meaning a fixed maximum number of A grades, is prohibited in undergraduate courses.

### Extra credit

Extra credit is optional. If offered, it must be offered to every student in the section on the same terms. Extra credit offered to one student who asks, and not announced to the class, is a fairness violation and is the second most common successful ground for a grade appeal.

Extra credit may not exceed five percent of the total course grade without chair approval.

### Midterm grades

Midterm grades are required for all 1000 and 2000 level undergraduate courses. They are an early warning tool and do not appear on the transcript.

| Term | Midterm grades due |
| --- | --- |
| Winter 2026 | Friday, February 27, 2026, 16:30 |
| Fall 2026 | Friday, October 16, 2026, 16:30 |

Report the grade the student would receive if the course ended that day. A grade of C- or lower triggers an automatic outreach from the student's advisor and, for first year students, from Student Success.

### Changing a grade after submission

Once final grades post, a grade is final except in four situations: a computational or clerical error, a portion of the work was not graded, an incomplete is being cleared, or a grade appeal was decided in the student's favour.

To change a grade, file a Grade Change Request in myTRU under Teaching, then Grade Changes. The form requires a written reason. The chair approves. For a change more than one term old, the dean also approves.

Grade changes are not available for work submitted after the term ended, for a student who did additional work to improve a grade, or because a student's student aid, study permit, or athletic eligibility depends on it. Requests on those grounds are declined and are logged.

Grade change requests filed more than one calendar year after the grade posted require Provost approval and are rarely granted.

### Student grade appeals

A student may appeal a final course grade. Individual assignment grades are not appealable on their own, only the final course grade.

**Grounds.** An appeal must claim at least one of the following. Disagreement with the instructor's academic judgment is not a ground, and an appeal that claims only this is dismissed at stage one.

1. The grade was calculated incorrectly.
2. The grade was assigned by criteria other than those in the course outline.
3. The grading standard applied to this student differed from the standard applied to the rest of the class.
4. The grade was influenced by factors unrelated to academic performance, including discrimination or retaliation.

**Stage one, the instructor.** The student contacts the instructor in writing within **fifteen business days** of the grade posting. The instructor responds in writing within **ten business days**. Most appeals end here, usually because the instructor explains the calculation or finds a genuine arithmetic error.

An instructor who does not respond within ten business days moves the appeal to stage two automatically.

**Stage two, the department chair.** If unresolved, the student files with the chair within **ten business days** of the instructor's response. The chair reviews the course outline, the gradebook, and the student's submitted work, and may ask the instructor for a written statement and a copy of the grading records. The chair decides within **fifteen business days**.

The chair may not change a grade unilaterally. The chair may uphold the grade, direct the instructor to recompute, or refer the case to stage three.

**Stage three, the faculty Grade Review Committee.** The student files with the dean's office within **ten business days** of the chair's decision. A three member committee of faculty from outside the department reviews the record. The committee may interview both parties. It decides within **twenty business days**, and its decision is final.

Only the Grade Review Committee has the authority to change a grade over the instructor's objection, and it does so only when the record shows the grade was not arrived at by the course outline process.

**Total elapsed time** for a full three stage appeal is about eighty business days, roughly four months.

Students may bring a TRU Students' Union advocate to any stage.

### Records instructors must keep

Keep for **two calendar years** after the term ends:

- The course outline as delivered, including any mid term written changes
- The complete gradebook with all component scores
- Rubrics and answer keys used
- Returned work that students did not collect
- Written communications about grades and extensions

If a student appeals and the instructor cannot produce the gradebook, the appeal is normally decided for the student. This is the single most common reason instructors lose appeals.

Departing faculty must hand grading records to the department chair before their last working day.

### Practical guidance

- Publish the weighting of every component in the course outline, as percentages that add to one hundred.
- Keep the gradebook current in Moodle. A student who can see their standing all term rarely appeals.
- Return major work within two weeks so students can adjust before the withdrawal deadline.
- Write one sentence of rationale on any grade below C. It takes a minute and prevents most appeals.
- If you find you made an error, fix it immediately and tell the student you found it. That ends the matter.
- Never negotiate a grade in a conversation you do not document.

## Course Outline Requirements and Course Design Standards

Thompson Rivers University. Centre for Excellence in Learning and Teaching. Version 5.0, effective January 5, 2026.

Sample data. This document is demonstration content written for a prototype assistant. It is not real TRU policy. Confirm anything consequential with the responsible office.

The course outline is the contract between the instructor and the class. In a grade appeal or an integrity case, it is the first document anyone reads, and a claim that is not in the outline is usually not enforceable. This guide covers what must be in it, what should be in it, and how courses are designed and approved.

At TRU the document is called a course outline, not a syllabus. The two words mean the same thing here and both are understood.

### Required elements

An outline that is missing any of these is returned by the chair for revision.

1. **Course identification.** Subject code, number, section, credits, term, campus, meeting days, times, and room or online platform.
2. **Instructor information.** Name, office location, office hours with at least three scheduled hours per week for full time faculty, TRU email address, and expected response time. State the response time in plain terms, for example within two business days.
3. **Course description.** The calendar description, reproduced exactly. You may add your own paragraph after it, but you may not replace it.
4. **Prerequisites.** Listed exactly as in the calendar.
5. **Learning outcomes.** Between three and eight measurable outcomes. See the section below.
6. **Required materials.** Every text, code, subscription, software licence, lab kit, and clicker, with the full cost of each in Canadian dollars. If there is no cost, say so.
7. **Assessment structure.** Every graded component with its weight as a percentage. The percentages must total one hundred.
8. **Grading scale.** The letter cutoffs you will use, including any approved departure from the university standard.
9. **Late work policy.** Required even if the policy is that no late work is accepted.
10. **Attendance policy.** How attendance is taken, whether it affects the grade, and how much.
11. **Generative AI policy.** Required in every outline from Winter 2026. See below.
12. **Course schedule.** Topics and major due dates by week, reflecting the term's statutory holidays and reading break. It may be labelled subject to change.
13. **Final exam date and time.** From the published exam block table, not a time of your choosing.
14. **The four required university statements.** See below.

### The four required university statements

Copy these into every outline. Current versions are always available in myTRU under Teaching, then Course Outline Toolkit. Copying an old version from a previous term is the most common compliance error.

**1. Accessibility and accommodations.** Directs students to Accessibility Services, states that accommodations are not retroactive, and gives the contact details.

**2. Academic integrity.** Points to the Academic Integrity and Plagiarism Casework Guide, and states that violations are reported to the Office of Academic Integrity.

**3. Respectful learning environment and human rights.** States protected grounds under the BC Human Rights Code, names the Office of Safety and Human Rights, and includes the required note that instructors must report disclosures of sexual violence to the designated office.

**4. Student wellbeing and support.** Lists Counselling Services, the after hours crisis line, the TRU Students' Union food bank, Supporting Student Learning, and the Indigenous student support services in Cplul'kw'ten, the gathering place.

### Writing learning outcomes

An outcome describes what a student will be able to do, observably, by the end of the course. It is measurable if you can name the assignment that measures it.

Use verbs a grader can see: define, calculate, compare, analyse, design, critique, construct, evaluate, demonstrate, defend.

Avoid verbs no one can measure: understand, appreciate, know, be aware of, be familiar with, learn about.

Weak: Students will understand statistical inference.
Strong: Students will select and apply an appropriate hypothesis test to a dataset and interpret the result for a non technical reader.

Weak: Students will be familiar with modernist poetry.
Strong: Students will analyse a modernist poem using at least two critical frameworks and defend the reading in writing.

Every outcome must map to at least one graded assessment. The Curriculum Committee checks this mapping when a course is proposed or revised.

### Credit hours and student workload

One credit equals one hour of direct instruction plus two hours of student work outside class, each week, for a thirteen week term.

| Credits | Weekly contact time | Expected outside work | Total per week |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 50 minutes | 2 hours | about 3 hours |
| 3 | 150 minutes | 6 hours | about 9 hours |
| 4 | 200 minutes | 8 hours | about 12 hours |

A three credit course therefore assumes about one hundred and seventeen total student hours across the term.

Compressed, online, and Open Learning courses must meet the same total, not the same calendar. A seven week summer session covers a thirteen week workload in seven weeks, which is roughly seventeen hours per week for a three credit course. Design accordingly and say so in the outline.

Estimating reading load: dense scholarly text runs six to eight pages per hour, textbook prose about twelve to fifteen pages per hour, and narrative or fiction about thirty pages per hour. Twelve hundred pages of scholarly reading in one term is not a three credit course.

### Generative AI policy statements

A statement is required in every outline. It is also the document that decides whether an AI integrity case can proceed at all, since a case cannot be sustained where the outline was silent.

Say four things: what is allowed, what is not, how to disclose, and what happens if the policy is broken.

**Prohibited use model.** Generative AI may not be used for any graded work in this course. Submitting AI generated text, code, or images as your own is a violation of the academic integrity policy and will be reported.

**Permitted with disclosure model.** You may use generative AI for brainstorming, outlining, and revising your own drafts. You may not use it to produce text you submit as your own. Any use must be disclosed in a short note at the end of the assignment naming the tool and what you used it for. Undisclosed use is a violation.

**Integrated use model.** Generative AI is a required tool in this course. You will use it for the tasks named in each assignment, and you will submit your prompts and the model output alongside your own analysis. You remain responsible for every claim in your submission, including the accuracy of all citations.

Whichever model you choose, avoid two traps. Do not name a detector tool as your enforcement method, because detector output is not accepted as evidence. Do not write a policy so vague that nobody can tell what is prohibited, because a vague policy is unenforceable.

### Accessible course materials

Accessibility is a design requirement, not an accommodation request. These are the minimum standards for every course.

- **Documents.** Use real heading styles rather than large bold text. Every image needs alternative text. Tables need header rows. Do not distribute scanned images of text, because a screen reader cannot read them. Run the accessibility checker before uploading.
- **Slides.** Use the built in layouts. Minimum twenty four point body text. Do not carry meaning by colour alone.
- **Video.** Every video must be captioned. Auto captions are a starting point and must be corrected. Learning Technology provides captioning at no charge to the department, with a five business day turnaround. Submit requests to learningtech@tru.ca.
- **Audio.** Provide a transcript.
- **Colour contrast.** At least 4.5 to 1 for body text.
- **Links.** Use descriptive link text. Do not write click here.
- **PDFs.** Must be text based and tagged. A scanned PDF is not accessible and must be run through optical character recognition first.

Learning Technology audits a random sample of Moodle course sites each term and sends findings privately to the instructor, not to the chair.
